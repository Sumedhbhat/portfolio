01 / ConclusionThe work was stronger than the resume.

The original resume had a signal-extraction problem, not an experience problem. It presented Sumedh as a capable developer who completed many features. The repository evidence supports a stronger story: an SDE II who owns multi-tenant platform, security, deployment, migration, and reliability problems across Angular, .NET, and Azure.

30%faster monorepo builds
10%smaller application bundles
25%higher automated test coverage
100+properties in portfolio scope

Revised positioning: Software Engineer II building multi-tenant analytics platforms with end-to-end ownership across architecture, authorization, migrations, CI/CD, testing, and operations.

The remaining weakness is measurement. The next improvement should come from collecting truthful scale, operational, business, and leadership evidence rather than adding more technologies or adjectives.

02 / MethodA bounded public benchmark, not an employee directory.

No complete and reliable public collection contains every resume of every engineer at MAANG companies. Many engineers publish nothing, public pages can be stale, and titles are not perfectly comparable across companies. Private LinkedIn data was not scraped.

The analysis uses:

  1. Public PDFs, structured resumes, and resume-like pages voluntarily published by engineers identifying SDE II, Senior, or adjacent experience.
  2. A small upper-bound group of Staff and Architect examples used only to calibrate senior signals.
  3. Official job descriptions from Amazon, Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Uber.
  4. The LaTeX resume and two local contribution summaries covering August 2024 through August 2026.

Validation boundary: the linked documents were opened and inspected. Employment, titles, dates, and metrics remain self-reported and were not independently verified with employers.

04 / PatternsWhat strong engineering resumes repeatedly demonstrate.

Scope appears early

Users, customers, tenants, devices, requests, events, data, revenue, teams, or products make the problem boundary legible.

Ownership is concrete

"Owned" or "led" is followed by an API, migration, architecture, rollout, on-call area, roadmap, or standard.

Engineering causes an outcome

Business impact, delivery speed, reliability, cost, customer experience, operational load, or risk reduction explains why the work mattered.

Production responsibility is visible

On-call, runbooks, alerting, SLAs, failure handling, migrations, incident response, and rollback controls distinguish production engineering.

Senior engineers multiply others

Mentoring, design reviews, interviews, standards, reusable frameworks, and migration playbooks show influence beyond individual tickets.

Selection beats completeness

Recent work gets detail; internships, generic skills, and duplicate project explanations are compressed.

Owned a meaningful problem + made a technically credible change + caused an observable result.

05 / Hiring barOfficial roles reward broad ownership, not framework count.

  • Amazon SDE II: architecture, components, availability, security, latency, microservices, mentoring, and operations.
  • Uber SWE II: code quality, reviews, tests, documentation, and the requirements-to-monitor lifecycle.
  • Google Senior, Global Infrastructure: distributed systems, technical leadership, mentoring, and large-scale infrastructure.
  • Google One Security Senior: technical direction, security standards, adoption, and cross-team collaboration.
  • Microsoft Bengaluru Senior: long-term strategy, complex architecture, influence, and cross-organization delivery.
  • Apple Senior, AI Data Platform: platform architecture, production AI, distributed systems, secure MCP/OAuth, troubleshooting, and stakeholders.
  • Uber Senior: scalable systems, collaborative role modeling, thought leadership, and mentorship.

The shared bar is broad ownership under production constraints. No single language, framework, or cloud provider substitutes for it.

07 / GapsThe evidence still missing from the resume.

Priority 0 Collect before serious Senior applications

  1. System scale: client organizations, tenants, workspaces, datasets, dashboards, reports, users, deployments, requests, events, rows, or regions.
  2. Business or operational results: onboarding time, manual steps, support tickets, deployment lead time, failed deployments, hours saved, adoption, or retention.
  3. Production ownership: on-call, incidents, RCAs, SLO/SLA, alerts, dashboards, MTTR, rollback, or recovery testing.
  4. Technical leadership: RFCs, design documents, reviews, architectural decisions, migration sequencing, roadmap influence, or adopted standards.
  5. Multiplier effect: mentoring, onboarding, code/design reviews, interviewing, talks, documentation, playbooks, or reusable libraries.

Priority 1 Strengthen the SDE II story

  1. Make the PM, design, QA, Sales, Security, BI/data, platform, and customer-team boundaries explicit.
  2. Document orchestration details such as retry, idempotency, cancellation, timeouts, batch isolation, concurrency, and consistency.
  3. Capture migration risk: backfills, compatibility, rollback, feature flags, environments, and tenants migrated.
  4. Add adoption for embed-only reports, bookmarks, dataset access, AI analytics, or client-workspace deployment.
  5. If Software Engineer II was a promotion, show the prior title and promotion date. Do not imply one if it was not.

Priority 2 Tailoring and polish

  • Tailor the first three bullets and skills order to each job.
  • Keep the published project while it remains differentiating and space-efficient.
  • Restore LeetCode or hackathon recognition only when it does not crowd out production evidence.
  • Include certifications only when current and materially relevant.

09 / StrategyOne strong SDE II version; a separate Senior stretch.

Primary: SDE II

Use the verified one-page resume for roles asking for roughly three to five years of experience. Lead with the correct title, current-role platform/security/deployment evidence, one published project, selective skills, and education.

Review the implemented resume changes

Stretch: Senior

Create a Senior version after proving at least three signals: multi-engineer design-to-operation leadership, technical design and rollout ownership, on-call responsibility, mentoring or adopted standards, two quantified outcomes, or roadmap influence.

Changing the headline to Senior without adding senior evidence will weaken credibility. The work may exist; it needs documentation.

11 / RevisionWhat changed in the resume.

  • Corrected the headline from Software Developer to Software Engineer II.
  • Removed the outdated generic summary; the title, stack, and first bullets now establish the narrative efficiently.
  • Rebuilt MRI Software bullets around reusable client-workspace deployment, tenancy/security, architecture, customer scope, automation, MCP self-service, and measured engineering leverage.
  • Compressed internships and removed duplicate Agora product description.
  • Consolidated JOTE around publication, 70% content reduction, and summaries in under five minutes.
  • Replaced low-signal IDEs and baseline concepts with Azure, Durable Functions, Power BI Embedded, GitHub automation, Nx/ESBuild, OAuth/OIDC, authorization, and multi-tenant SaaS.
  • Kept recognition in modular source files but omitted it from the primary PDF so the result remains one page and experience-led.
  • Used only claims present in the original resume or supported by the contribution summaries. No business metrics were invented.

Final assessment: the revised document is suitable for SDE II applications and materially closer to the evidence pattern in successful top-company resumes. Senior applications still need scope, production ownership, technical leadership, and business or reliability outcomes.
